Do Things Really Happen for a Reason?

Personal Reflection

Sometimes I look back at my life and wonder how different it would have been if I had made just one decision differently.

What if I had chosen another path? What if I had never met a particular person? What if something I desperately wanted had actually happened?

One decision leads to another, and before we realize it, we find ourselves living a life that might have been entirely different.

At such moments, the familiar expression comes to mind: “Everything happens for a reason.”

I have heard these words countless times, particularly when something goes wrong. A relationship ends, a job opportunity disappears, or a carefully made plan fails.

“Perhaps it happened for a reason,” someone says.

There is comfort in that thought. It suggests that what disappoints us today might eventually make sense.

But I have begun to wonder whether we sometimes confuse two very different things: something happening for a reason and something happening because of reasons.

If I miss a flight because I leave home late, there is a reason I missed it. If a relationship ends because two people want different things, there are reasons behind the ending. Our lives are shaped by decisions, circumstances, timing, other people’s actions, and sometimes chance.

But saying that something happened for a reason suggests something more. It suggests that the event had a purpose.

Perhaps I missed the flight because I was not meant to be on it. Perhaps someone left my life because another person was supposed to enter it. Perhaps one opportunity disappeared because a better one was waiting.

How could I know whether any of that was true?

Imagine that I apply for a job I desperately want but do not get it. I am disappointed, so I continue looking. A year later, I find another position that changes my life for the better.

Looking back, I might say, “Now I understand. I didn't get that first job because I was meant to get this one.”

But perhaps I simply did not get the first job.

Because I was rejected, I continued searching. Because I continued searching, I found another opportunity. And because I accepted it, my life took a different direction.

The rejection certainly influenced what happened next. But does that mean it was deliberately placed in my path?

I don't know.

And perhaps “I don't know” is an answer we should be more comfortable with.

I do not want to dismiss the remarkable way life sometimes unfolds. A chance meeting can lead to a lifelong relationship. An unexpected delay can put us in a place we never intended to visit. A disappointment can make us reconsider what we want.

Years later, these events can seem to fit together almost perfectly.

But looking backward is different from looking forward.

When we are living through an experience, we cannot see all the paths that might open afterward. We make decisions with the information available to us, often without knowing where those decisions will lead.

Only later do we begin connecting the events into a story.

We say, “If that had not happened, I would never have come here.”

That can be true.

But it is different from saying, “That happened so that I would come here.”

The first statement describes a connection. The second assumes a purpose.

There is another reason I hesitate to believe that everything happens for a reason.

Some experiences are simply terrible.

When someone loses a loved one, suffers violence, or experiences a devastating injustice, telling them that everything happens for a reason can suggest that their suffering was somehow necessary.

Do we really need to believe that something terrible had to happen so that something good could eventually follow?

I don't think so.

Sometimes painful things happen, and no beautiful explanation appears afterward. We do not have to justify suffering in order to acknowledge what someone learns or does after experiencing it.

Yet I believe something important is possible.

We can create meaning from experiences that did not necessarily arrive with a purpose.

Someone who has experienced loss might become more understanding of another person's grief. Someone who has failed might discover the courage to try again. Someone whose plans collapse might find a direction they would never have considered.

That does not mean the loss, failure, or disappointment needed to happen.

It means that what happens to us does not always determine what we do next.

I sometimes imagine life as a road. We choose a direction, believing we know where it leads. Then something blocks our way.

Perhaps nobody placed that obstacle there to guide us toward a better destination. But because the road is blocked, we turn. We discover another route. We meet different people and make different choices.

Years later, we might look back and realize how much that unexpected turn changed our lives.

We still cannot know whether the journey was planned in advance.

But we can recognize the meaning we found along the way.

So I no longer hear “Everything happens for a reason” quite as I once did.

Things certainly happen because of reasons. Whether every event has a predetermined purpose is something I cannot know.

What I do believe is that something does not have to happen for a reason for us to find a reason to move forward from it.

Perhaps meaning is not always hidden inside the events of our lives, waiting to be discovered.

Sometimes, we create it through what we choose to do next.
